\large Exact FP16 for the Few Tiles That Matter, FP4 for the Rest}
\author{Raphael Friedmann\\\small\url{https://github.com/kekzl/apa}}
\date{October 2026, APA 0.6.1}

\begin{abstract}
In long-context causal prefill, the softmax mass of a query row concentrates in a small number of key tiles.
APA is a causal flash-attention prefill kernel for the RTX 5090 (sm\_120a) that exploits this: a first pass
runs block-scaled FP4 attention (\code{mma.sync kind::mxf4nvf4}) over every 64-key tile and flags a tile as
\emph{hot} when its share of the running row sum exceeds a threshold $\eps$. Hot tiles skip the FP4
$PV$ product and are recomputed by a second pass in exact FP16; the two partial results are merged by a
log-sum-exp. Both passes run in one fused launch with persistent pass-2 workers. A hot tile must not set the
reference maximum of the FP4 accumulation: when an attention sink does, the remaining probabilities fall
below the FP4 scale range and round to zero. Keeping the cold frame at most $2^{32}$ below hot maxima raises
the minimum cosine similarity over all 49152 (row, head) pairs of the worst of six Llama-3.2-3B attention
dumps from $-0.638155$ to 0.841302 and removes all 899 pairs with negative cosine (six dumps), at unchanged speed. The
rows that remain worst lose their accuracy to FP4 rounding of $Q$, not of $K$, $P$ or $V$ (host emulation per
error source); a second FP4 term of $Q$, UE4M3 instead of power-of-two $P$ scales and block scales of least
squared error remove all 137 pairs below cosine 0.9 on twelve dumps and raise the worst minimum from 0.773144 to
0.984284, at 6.7 to 25.4\,\% more attention time. At
$\eps=0.005$ and 122880 keys, 6.0 to 10.3\,\% of active tiles are hot and the attention kernel of APA 0.3.0 runs
2.50$\times$ faster than the same kernel with every tile exact (2.196$\times$ with the 0.730\,ms quantization
step included); the speedup grows with context from 1.30$\times$ at 8192 keys. Integrated into the imp inference engine (version 0.2.0 of the kernel), dense
prefill of a 106451-token prompt drops from 11659.35\,ms (FlashAttention-2) to 6433.23\,ms (1.81$\times$),
with perplexity below FlashAttention-2 with FP16 or FP32 accumulation on Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen3-8B. The kernel is deterministic (bitwise identical across reruns), supports GQA, paged
KV caches and chunked prefill with an incremental tile cache, and is released as a header-only CUDA library
under MPL-2.0.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Prefill cost of long prompts is dominated by attention: for a prompt of length $N$ the work is
$O(N^2 d)$ while the rest of the model is $O(N d^2)$. FlashAttention \cite{dao2022flashattention,
dao2024flashattention2,shah2024flashattention3} removes the memory bottleneck, but the arithmetic remains.
Two families of methods attack it. \emph{Low-precision} attention \cite{zhang2025sageattention,
zhang2025sageattention2,zhang2025sageattention3} computes $QK^\top$ and $PV$ in INT8, FP8 or FP4 with
smoothing and fine-grained scales; it keeps every key but adds error to every score. \emph{Sparse}
attention \cite{xiao2024streamingllm,jiang2024minference,tang2024quest} skips keys predicted to carry little
mass; it is exact on the kept keys and wrong on the skipped ones.

APA combines both at tile granularity and lets the data decide the split per query block. Every tile is
computed cheaply in FP4, so no key is dropped. The few tiles that carry a large share of the row's softmax
mass, where FP4 error would dominate the output, are routed to an exact FP16 pass. The decision is made
online inside the FP4 pass from quantities the pass computes anyway, so it costs one extra small MMA per tile.

Contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item A two-pass attention scheme with an online hot-tile test whose cold verdicts are monotone safe: the
  running row sum only grows, so a tile judged cold stays below $\eps$ of the final row sum, both as estimated
  in FP4 (Section~\ref{sec:hot}).
\item A pass-1 tile order (sink tile first, then the diagonal backwards) that puts high-mass tiles into the
  running sum early, so the test judges middle tiles against a near-final denominator.
\item A cold reference frame decoupled from hot tiles, with a diagnosis of the failure it removes: rows whose
  output lives in the low-mass tail lost that tail to FP4 underflow next to a dominant sink
  (Sections~\ref{sec:coldframe} and~\ref{sec:diag}).
\item An FP4 pass on sm\_120 block-scaled MMA with mean-centred K, per-16-key power-of-two $P$ scales folded
  into the exponent, a lazy row maximum, and row sums obtained from the same quantized $P$ via an MMA
  against a ones tile.
\item A single fused launch: pass-1 CTAs followed by persistent pass-2 workers that pull work from ticket
  counters and wait on per-query-block release flags, deadlock-free by ticket order.
\item An error-source analysis of the remaining worst rows (FP4 rounding of $Q$, then of $P$, at short context of
  $K$), fixed by a two-term FP4 $Q$, UE4M3 $P$ scales and block scales of least squared error
  (Section~\ref{sec:qerr}).
\item Deterministic statistics and an incremental tile cache for chunked prefill over paged KV.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}

\paragraph{Online softmax.} For a query row $q$ and keys $k_j$, flash attention keeps a running maximum $m$,
row sum $\ell=\sum_j e^{s_j-m}$ and unnormalized output $o=\sum_j e^{s_j-m}v_j$, rescaling $\ell$ and $o$
when $m$ grows \cite{milakov2018online,rabe2021selfattention}. Two partial states $(m_1,\ell_1,o_1)$ and
$(m_2,\ell_2,o_2)$ over disjoint key sets merge exactly:
\begin{equation}
M=\max(m_1,m_2),\quad w_i=e^{m_i-M},\quad O=\frac{w_1o_1+w_2o_2}{w_1\ell_1+w_2\ell_2}.
\label{eq:merge}
\end{equation}
APA uses this to merge the FP4 result over cold tiles with the FP16 result over hot tiles. All exponentials
run in the $\log_2$ domain (\code{ex2.approx}).

\paragraph{Block-scaled FP4 on sm\_120.} The RTX 5090 executes
block-scaled MMA (\code{mma.sync}, \code{kind::mxf4nvf4}, m16n8k64) with E2M1 operands and one UE4M3 scale per 16 elements
along $k$ (NVFP4, a variant of the OCP microscaling formats \cite{rouhani2023microscaling}). E2M1 represents
$\{0,\pm0.5,\pm1,\pm1.5,\pm2,\pm3,\pm4,\pm6\}$; UE4M3 has a maximum of 448. APA additionally uses a global FP32
scale per head so that $|x|_{\max}$ maps to $6\cdot448=2688$.

\section{Method}

APA computes causal attention for head dim $d=128$ with GQA ($H$ query heads, $H_{kv}$ KV heads,
$G=H/H_{kv}$). Keys are split into tiles of $B_{kv}=64$. Query rows of the $G$ heads sharing a KV head are
\emph{packed}: packed row $r$ is position $\lfloor r/G\rfloor$ of query head $h_{kv}G+(r\bmod G)$, so one CTA
of 12 warps covers $12\cdot16=192$ packed rows and loads each KV tile once for all $G$ heads.

\paragraph{Mean-centred K.} Subtracting the per-channel key mean, $s_j=q\cdot(k_j-\bar k)$, shifts every
score of a row by the same constant $q\cdot\bar k$ and leaves the softmax unchanged
\cite{zhang2025sageattention}. It removes the shared channel offset that would otherwise consume FP4 range.
Pass 2 computes exact scores $q\cdot k_j$ from the original FP16 keys and subtracts $q\cdot\bar k$, so both
passes share one logit frame and Eq.~\eqref{eq:merge} applies.

\subsection{Pass 1: FP4 attention with a hot-tile test}
\label{sec:hot}

Each warp owns 16 packed rows; a CTA of 12 warps streams the tile blobs through a 6-stage shared-memory ring
filled by \code{cp.async.bulk} with mbarrier completion (thread 0 is the producer). Per tile and warp:

\begin{figure}[t]
\small
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item $S \gets \mathrm{MMA_{fp4}}(Q, K_t)$; apply causal / tail mask
\item candidate max $c \gets m$, or the row max if a score exceeds $m+\tau$
\item per 16-key group: power-of-two scale $2^{k}$, $P' \gets 2^{s c' + b}$ in frame $c$, $P'\le6$
\item $\ell_t \gets \mathrm{MMA_{fp4}}(P', \mathbf{1})$ \hfill tile row sums, frame $c$
\item if any row has $\ell_t > \eps\,(2^{m-c}\lambda+\ell_t)$: hot
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item $f \gets \max(m,\, c-\delta)$; move $o$, $\ell_{\text{cold}}$, $\lambda$ to frame $f$
  \item $\lambda \gets \lambda + 2^{c-f}\ell_t$; set bit $t$ in the hot mask, stop
  \end{itemize}
\item cold: move $o$, $\ell_{\text{cold}}$, $\lambda$ to frame $c$; $\lambda \gets \lambda+\ell_t$
\item $\ell_{\text{cold}} \gets \ell_{\text{cold}} + \ell_t$;\; $o \gets o + \mathrm{MMA_{fp4}}(P', V_t)$
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{P quantization.} The probabilities of a 16-key group are scaled by $1536/s$, where $s$ is the smallest
UE4M3 value with $s\ge256\cdot\max_{\text{group}}P$: with $u$ the required $\log_2 s$, $m=\lceil 8(2^{u-\lfloor u\rfloor}-1)\rceil$
and $s=2^{\lfloor u\rfloor}(1+m/8)$ (3 mantissa bits; $m=8$ carries into the exponent). The quantized values satisfy
$P'\le6$, and the group maximum lands near 6 instead of anywhere in $(3,6]$ as with a power of two (0.4.0). The
shift $\lfloor u\rfloor+\log_2(1+m/8)$ is folded into the bias of the exponent, so \code{ex2} directly produces $P'$;
the global factor 1536 is divided out in the epilogue.

\paragraph{Two-term Q.} $Q$ is quantized twice: $Q_1$ as in Table~\ref{tab:prep} and $Q_2$, the E2M1 residual $Q-Q_1$ in the
same row scale, so $S=Q_1K^\top+Q_2K^\top$ accumulates in one fragment with the same K operands, at 16 more OMMAs
per tile. Section~\ref{sec:qerr} shows why: FP4 rounding of $Q$, not of $K$, $P$ or $V$, decided the worst rows;
with two terms, $P$ became the largest error source, which the UE4M3 scale above addresses.

\paragraph{Block scales.} Every block of 16 values the prep quantizes ($Q_1$, $Q_2$, K, $V^\top$) takes the UE4M3
scale code among $c_0+\{0,\pm1,-2,3,4,5,6\}$, $c_0$ the code nearest to $\max|x|/6$, with the least squared error
after an E2M1 round trip. The nearest code can round the scale down, which clips the block maximum at 6, or up,
which wastes part of the grid. At short context FP4 rounding of K decides the worst rows (Section~\ref{sec:qerr}).
The error per candidate comes from $x$ in f16x2 and one F2FP conversion each way
(\code{cvt.rn.satfinite.e2m1x2.f16x2}): prep for 2048 queries on 26624 keys takes 0.170 instead of 0.137\,ms, at
122880 keys 0.501 instead of 0.492\,ms; attention time is unchanged.

\paragraph{Lazy maximum.} The running maximum $m$ (log$_2$ domain) is updated, with a full quad reduction
and a rescale of $o$, $\ell_{\text{cold}}$ and $\lambda$, only when some score exceeds $m+\tau$ with
$\tau=4$. The bias absorbs the lag, so $P'\le6$ still holds; most tiles skip the rescale.

\paragraph{Row sums from the quantized P.} Instead of summing FP32 probabilities, pass 1 multiplies the packed
$P'$ fragment by a tile of E2M1 ones. The resulting row sums are those of the values that enter $PV$, so
numerator and denominator of the cold partial carry the same quantization.

\paragraph{The hot test.} $\lambda$ accumulates the sums of \emph{all} tiles seen so far, hot ones included,
while $\ell_{\text{cold}}$ and $o$ accumulate only cold ones. A tile is hot for the warp if, for any of its
16 rows, its row sum exceeds $\eps\lambda$. The test is warp-uniform (\code{\_\_any\_sync}), so the hot
mask has one bit per (warp, tile).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Proposition.} Let $\ell_t$ be pass 1's FP4 row sum of tile $t$, $\lambda_t$ the
running sum after tile $t$ and $\lambda_\infty$ the final sum, all in the frame of the final maximum. Every tile
$t$ that pass 1 marks cold satisfies $\ell_t\le\eps\lambda_\infty$, and the cold tiles together carry at most
$n_c\,\eps$ of $\lambda_\infty$, where $n_c$ is their number. The statement is about the FP4 estimates; the
FP32 share of a cold tile can exceed $\eps$ (Section~\ref{sec:diag}: up to 0.0118 at $\eps=0.005$).

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Proof.} A rescale multiplies $\ell_t$, $\lambda$ and all later sums by the same
factor, so shares are frame-invariant. Sums of exponentials are non-negative, hence $\lambda_t\le\lambda_\infty$
and $\ell_t\le\eps\lambda_t\le\eps\lambda_\infty$. Summing over cold tiles gives the second bound. $\square$

\smallskip The per-tile bound is what the test guarantees; the total bound $n_c\eps$ is loose (at 122880
keys, $n_c$ can approach 1920). A hot verdict can be conservative; it costs pass-2 work, not accuracy.
The FP4 error of the cold part is bounded only through the per-tile share, which is the mechanism behind
the worst-case rows in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\paragraph{Cold frame.}
\label{sec:coldframe}
The frame $m$ sets the scale of every later $P'$. The per-group exponent $k$ is clamped at $-6$ so that
$2^k$ stays a normal UE4M3 value; a group whose maximum lies more than about $2^{-10}$ below the frame
therefore quantizes to values under 0.25 and rounds to E2M1 zero. In APA 0.2.0 the frame was the running
maximum over all tiles. The sink tile, visited first and usually hot, then set $m$, and in rows whose
remaining mass is spread thinly (largest cold tile at most 0.0025 of the row) most cold groups vanished
(Section~\ref{sec:diag}).
Hot tiles do not need $m$: their $P'$ is used only for $\ell_t$, computed in the candidate frame $c$, and
pass 2 recomputes them. APA 0.3.0 therefore lets a hot tile lift the frame only to $\max(m, c-\delta)$ with
$\delta=32$; $\lambda$ absorbs the hot sum as $2^{c-f}\ell_t\le2^{32}\ell_t$, well inside FP32 range. A cold
tile commits its candidate as before; with $\delta=0$ the kernel reproduces the 0.2.0 metrics to all six
printed digits on six dumps. Frames are row-wise; the proposition above holds unchanged, since all sums still
share one frame. What the change guarantees: a cold group is quantized without the $-6$ clamp if its maximum
lies at most $2^{-10}$ below the frame, and a hot tile moves the frame to at most $2^{-32}$ of its own
maximum, so a hot tile can push a cold group to zero only if that group lies more than $2^{-42}$ below the
hot tile's maximum. Nothing bounds the FP4 error of the cold mass that remains.

\paragraph{Tile order.} The denominator $\lambda$ is small early in the loop, which makes early tiles look
hot. Pass 1 therefore visits tile 0 first (the attention sink \cite{xiao2024streamingllm}), then the
remaining tiles from the causal diagonal backwards. The tiles that typically carry most mass enter $\lambda$
first, and the long middle range is judged against a near-final sum.

\paragraph{Epilogue.} A warp without hot tiles normalizes and stores its output directly. A warp with hot
tiles exports $(m,\ell_{\text{cold}})$ and the unnormalized cold partial $o$ in FP32 for the merge.

\subsection{Pass 2: exact FP16 over hot tiles}

A pass-2 CTA takes the 192 packed rows of one pass-1 query block with the same 12 warps. The union of their
hot tiles streams from the original FP16 K and V (flat or paged) through a ring of 3 shared-memory stages of
one K and one V tile: any warp that finds the next stage released by all warps claims it (atomic CAS) and
fills it with \code{cp.async}, completion signalled on an mbarrier. Every warp passes every union step, so
the 12 warps share each load; against three independent 4-warp groups (0.3.0) this cuts attention time by
1.2 to 3.1\,\% at $\eps=0.005$ and 0.01 on four dumps, with bitwise equal outputs. Each warp computes only its own hot tiles with
\code{mma.sync.m16n8k16} (f16 inputs, f16 accumulation, the default of imp's FlashAttention-2 path) and an
FP32 online softmax in the $\log_2$ domain. At the end each row is merged with its pass-1 partial by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:merge} and written to $O$.

The output accumulator stays in f16 across tiles by default. With few hot tiles this is harmless; with all
tiles hot ($\eps<0$), small late contributions next to a dominant sink round away
(Section~\ref{sec:diag}). \code{APA\_P2\_F32O=1} sums each 16-token k-step in f16 at full HMMA rate and adds
it to an FP32 accumulator; the extra 32 registers per thread cause spills in the 384-thread fused kernel.

\subsection{Fused launch}

Both passes run in one launch of $n_1+n_{\text{SM}}$ CTAs of 384 threads, where $n_1$ is the number of
(batch, KV head, query block) pass-1 items. Each CTA takes a ticket from an atomic counter. Tickets below
$n_1$ run pass 1 (causal: heaviest query blocks first); the rest become persistent pass-2 workers that
pull query blocks from a second counter. A pass-2 worker spins on its
query block's ready flag, which the pass-1 CTA sets with a release store after a fence; the worker reads it
with acquire semantics. Tickets are handed to CTAs in the order they start running, so every pass-1 item a
worker waits on is already resident: the scheme cannot deadlock. Pass-2 work fills the SMs left idle by
pass 1's last wave.

\subsection{Determinism}

All floating-point reductions run in a fixed order. In 0.1.0 the K column sums used shared and global float
atomics; 5 reruns of prep and attention on the three dumps differed in 539072, 1329742 and 950802 output
elements. In 0.2.0 each 16-tile chunk sums its columns in registers, then by XOR shuffles, then over warps in
order, and the finalize kernel sums chunks in order. Maxima use integer \code{atomicMax}, which is
order-independent. The same 5 reruns now give bitwise identical outputs; prep time fell from 0.865 to
0.731\,ms at 122880 keys.

\subsection{Paged KV and chunked prefill}

Pass 2 reads K/V through a reader abstraction: flat \code{[B][S][H\_kv][d]} or a paged FP16 pool through a
block table \cite{kwon2023pagedattention}, plus a flat tail for the chunk not yet in the cache. For chunked
prefill, \code{prefill\_incremental} keeps a per-layer \code{KvState} with the quantized tile blobs and
statistics; a new chunk quantizes only its new keys. K mean and head scales stay frozen until the context
has grown by a factor \code{KV\_RESTAT} (1.125, 2 in 0.3.0); then every tile is requantized. Head scales are
widened by a headroom factor so later keys fit, and a fingerprint of 48 key values detects a stale cache and triggers a full requantization.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\paragraph{Setup.} RTX 5090 (sm\_120a), CUDA 13. Standalone runs use attention inputs dumped from
Llama-3.2-3B \cite{grattafiori2024llama3} (24 query heads, 8 KV heads, $d=128$): 2048 query rows against
122880 keys (lc\_122880\_\{0,1,2\}) or 32768 keys (lc\_32768\_\{0,1,2\}). Accuracy is the cosine similarity
against an FP32 reference, in two modes. \emph{All pairs}: every one of the $2048\cdot24=49152$ (row, head)
pairs; we report the mean and minimum of the per-pair cosines and the number of pairs below 0.9 and 0.
\emph{Sample}: 512 pairs drawn with a fixed seed (1.04\,\%); ``cos'' is then the pooled cosine of all
sampled outputs concatenated and ``min'' the sample minimum, which overstates the all-pairs minimum on every
dump (Table~\ref{tab:cold}). Tables state their mode. Times are the minimum over 5 repetitions of the mean
of 5 launches (CUDA events) of the fused attention kernel; the quantization step (prep) is timed separately
and excluded unless stated. Absolute times drift by up to 10\,\%
between runs on this GPU (lc\_122880\_1 at $\eps=0.005$: 4.707 and 5.200\,ms in two runs), so every
comparison below is taken within one run. Hot share is the fraction of active (warp, tile) pairs flagged
hot. The kernel is deterministic, so accuracy figures do not vary between runs.

Table~\ref{tab:cold} compares 0.2.0 ($\delta=0$) with 0.3.0 ($\delta=32$) in one run. The minimum over all

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Mass is not output.} The hot test bounds each cold tile's FP4 share of the softmax mass, not
  of the output. The worst pair of 0.3.0 over all pairs (lc\_122880\_2, position 1370, head 18, cosine
  0.841302) has the shape of Table~\ref{tab:diag}: 3 hot tiles, the largest with 0.9101 of the mass, FP32
  cold mass 0.0794, and a cold output component 1.918 times the reference norm, so FP4 error on the tail
  decides the result. A test weighted by the value norm of a tile, or a bound on the accumulated cold mass,
  would target such rows. In 0.6.1 the worst pair of twelve dumps has cosine 0.984284 (lc\_122880\_0), which
  also has the most pairs below 0.99 (887).
\item \textbf{Chunked prefill.} The incremental tile cache keeps the K mean of its last restat; on
  lc\_122880\_2 the pairs below 0.9 grow from 24 to 431 as the context grows to 1.84 times the keys of that mean
  (1.11 times: 56; single-term $Q$; with two-term $Q$ the last chunk has none). \code{KV\_RESTAT} 1.125 caps the growth for 2.6\,\% more time; a per-restat K mean with a per-row score
  offset would remove it.
\item \textbf{Exact mode.} The f16 output accumulation of pass 2 limits the all-exact configuration;
  the FP32 alternative costs 14.5 to 24.9\,\% at $\eps=0.005$ through register spills.
\item \textbf{Granularity.} The hot verdict is per (warp, tile): one row of 16 makes the tile exact for all.
\item \textbf{Scope.} Head dim 128, causal, FP16 inputs, sm\_120a only.
\item \textbf{Evidence.} Six dumps from one model; accuracy over all pairs, times from single runs
  (drift up to 10\,\%); imp numbers come from the imp repository. A direct
  comparison with FP4 attention kernels such as SageAttention3 \cite{zhang2025sageattention3} on the same
  inputs is open.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}

FlashAttention \cite{dao2022flashattention,dao2024flashattention2,shah2024flashattention3} established
IO-aware exact attention; FlashAttention-3 adds FP8 on Hopper. SageAttention
\cite{zhang2025sageattention,zhang2025sageattention2,zhang2025sageattention3} quantizes Q, K, P, V to INT8,
INT4, FP8 and FP4 with K smoothing by mean subtraction, which APA adopts; it applies one precision to every
tile. StreamingLLM \cite{xiao2024streamingllm} keeps sink and recent tokens, MInference
\cite{jiang2024minference} predicts sparse patterns for prefill, Quest \cite{tang2024quest} selects pages by
query-aware bounds for decode. These skip keys; APA keeps every key and spends precision instead of
dropping work, with the split decided online from the FP4 pass itself.

\section{Conclusion}

Long-context attention is skewed enough that exact arithmetic on 6 to 10\,\% of the (warp, tile) pairs at 122880
keys, FP4 on the rest, and an exact log-sum-exp merge make the attention kernel 2.50$\times$ faster than an
all-exact kernel (2.196$\times$ with quantization). The
skew that makes this pay also creates the main hazard: a dominant sink can push the informative tail out of
